Section 1 — Must-Know Core (Almost Every Trader Uses These)
1
Relative Strength IndexRSI Momentum★ Bounce
▼

Measures how fast and how much a stock's price has changed recently. Outputs a single number between 0 and 100. The most popular single indicator in trading — used to spot when a stock is stretched too far in one direction and may reverse.

Parameters — RSI(period)
RSI(14)Default and most common. Looks back 14 candles (days on a daily chart, hours on hourly, etc.). The number 14 is the lookback window.
RSI(7)Shorter lookback. More sensitive — reacts faster but fires more false signals. Used by day traders.
RSI(21)Longer lookback. Smoother, fewer signals, more reliable. Used by swing and position traders.

Rule: Shorter number = faster but noisier. Longer number = slower but more reliable. The 14 is a balance most traders have agreed on.

RSI Chart — with 70 (overbought) and 30 (oversold) thresholds
RSI above 70
Overbought — stock may be due for a pullback or sell-off
RSI below 30
Oversold — stock may be due for a bounce or recovery
RSI 40–60
Neutral — no strong signal in either direction
RSI at 28, curling up
Leaving oversold territory — potential bounce entry trigger
Real example: A stock drops hard for 3 days. RSI(14) hits 24. That means the last 14 days of selling pressure has been extreme. Bounce traders watch for RSI to curl back up from below 30. The cross back above 30 is the entry trigger.
★ Bounce Trading: RSI is your #1 tool. Look for RSI dipping below 30, then crossing back above it. That crossback is your trigger. Always combine with volume to confirm the reversal is real and not a fake-out.
2
MACDMoving Average Convergence Divergence Trend★ Bounce
▼

Shows the relationship between two exponential moving averages of price. Gives you trend direction and momentum in one indicator. Has three parts on the chart: the MACD line, the signal line, and the histogram bars that show the gap between them.

Parameters — MACD(fast, slow, signal) — Three numbers separated by commas
MACD(12,26,9)Default and most common setting used by virtually all platforms.
12Fast EMA period — the shorter, more reactive moving average. Reacts quickly to recent price moves.
26Slow EMA period — the longer, base moving average. Shows the larger trend direction.
9Signal line period — an EMA of the MACD line itself. Smooths it out so crossovers are cleaner.

The MACD line = 12 EMA minus 26 EMA. When that difference grows, momentum is building. When it shrinks, momentum is fading.

MACD — MACD line (blue), Signal line (orange), Histogram (bars)
MACD crosses above signal
Bullish crossover — potential buy signal
MACD crosses below signal
Bearish crossover — potential sell signal
Histogram bars growing
Momentum increasing in current direction
Histogram bars shrinking
Momentum fading — possible trend change ahead
Real example: Stock has been falling. MACD and its signal line are both below zero. The histogram bars start getting smaller (less negative). Then MACD crosses above the signal line. This is a momentum shift — the setup bounce traders watch for.
★ Bounce Trading: Watch for MACD crossing above its signal line while both are still below zero. Oversold territory + momentum shift = strong bounce setup. Best when combined with RSI below 30 at the same time.
3–4
Moving AveragesSMA & EMA Trend★ Bounce
▼

SMA (Simple Moving Average) adds the closing prices over N days and divides by N — equal weight to every day. EMA (Exponential Moving Average) does the same thing but gives more weight to recent prices, so it reacts faster. Both create a smooth line on your chart that shows trend direction.

Parameters — SMA(period) or EMA(period) — one number = how many candles to average
SMA(20) / EMA(20)Short-term trend. Used by active traders to track recent price momentum.
SMA(50) / EMA(50)Medium-term trend. One of the most-watched lines on any chart. Institutions react at this level.
SMA(200) / EMA(200)Long-term benchmark. Above it = bull market territory. Below it = bear market territory.

EMA reacts faster than SMA with the same period. For the same number, EMA will move sooner when price changes direction. Active traders usually prefer EMA.

Price with SMA(50) and SMA(200) — Golden Cross and Death Cross zones shown
Price above SMA(200)
Long-term uptrend — generally safer to look for buys
Price below SMA(200)
Long-term downtrend — be cautious with buy entries
SMA(50) crosses above SMA(200)
"Golden Cross" — major bullish signal watched by all traders
SMA(50) crosses below SMA(200)
"Death Cross" — major bearish signal
★ Bounce Trading: EMA(20) and EMA(50) are your dynamic support zones. When price drops to touch EMA and RSI is near 30–40, and volume is low on the pullback — that is a textbook bounce entry zone. SMA(200) is the biggest floor of all; bounces off it are powerful.
5
Bollinger BandsBB Volatility★ Bounce
▼

Three lines drawn around price: a middle SMA, and upper and lower bands placed a set number of standard deviations away. The bands expand when volatility is high and contract when it is low. Price stays within the bands roughly 95% of the time, so touching a band is statistically meaningful.

Parameters — BB(period, standard deviations) — two numbers
BB(20, 2)Default. Middle = SMA(20). Bands are 2 standard deviations above and below the middle line.
20The lookback period for the middle SMA — how many days are averaged to draw the center line.
2Number of standard deviations for the band width. Higher = wider bands, fewer "touches." Lower = tighter, more signals but more false ones.
BB(20, 1.5)Tighter bands — used in scalping. More signals but more noise.
Bollinger Bands — Price (black), Upper Band (red), Middle SMA (blue), Lower Band (green)
Price touches upper band
Statistically stretched high — possible pullback or reversal
Price touches lower band
Statistically stretched low — possible bounce or reversal
Bands squeeze together
"Bollinger Squeeze" — low volatility, big move incoming (direction unknown)
Bands suddenly widen
Volatility expansion — a breakout or breakdown is in progress
Real example: Stock falls sharply and closes below the lower Bollinger Band. RSI is at 26. Next candle closes back inside the bands. This "band snap-back" pattern is a classic bounce signal. The lower band acts like a rubber band — price gets pulled back to the middle SMA.
★ Bounce Trading: A close below the lower band followed by a close back inside = strong bounce signal. Add RSI below 30 simultaneously for extra confirmation. This combination is one of the most reliable bounce setups available.
Section 2 — High Trader Agreement
6
VolumeVOL Volume★ Bounce
▼

The total number of shares traded in a period. Not a formula — a raw count. But it is the most important confirmation tool in trading. A price move without volume behind it is weak and untrustworthy. Volume reveals what is actually happening under the surface.

Volume has no formula input — compared against moving averages of itself:
Average VolumeUsually a 20-day or 50-day average. If today's volume is 2x the average, that is significant.
Relative Volume (RVOL)Today's volume ÷ average volume. RVOL of 2.0 = twice the normal trading activity today.
Volume bars — green = up day, red = down day. Watch for spikes on reversal days.
Price up + high volume
Strong buying — move is confirmed, likely to continue
Price up + low volume
Weak buying — may not hold, possible fake-out
Price drops on low volume
Weak selling — sellers not committed, bounce more likely
Price drops on high volume
Heavy selling — downtrend likely continues, avoid catching falling knives
★ Bounce Trading: The reversal candle must have noticeably higher volume than the down candles before it. A bounce on low volume is a trap. A bounce on high volume is an opportunity. Volume is your final confirmation before entering.

Stochastic OscillatorStoch Momentum
▼

Compares where the closing price is relative to the highest and lowest prices over a set period. Gives two lines — %K (fast) and %D (slow). Works similarly to RSI but focuses on the price range rather than the speed of change. Generates a crossover signal when the two lines intersect.

Parameters — Stoch(%K period, %K smoothing, %D period) — three numbers
Stoch(14,3,3)Default. %K looks back 14 periods, is smoothed 3 times, %D is a 3-period average of %K.
14How many periods to look back to find the high/low price range
3 (first)Smoothing applied to %K line to reduce noise and false signals
3 (second)Period for %D line — a moving average of %K. The signal crossovers happen between %K and %D.
Stochastic Oscillator — %K (blue), %D (orange), with 80/20 overbought/oversold zones
%K above 80
Overbought — possible pullback coming
%K below 20
Oversold — watch for reversal signals
%K crosses above %D below 20
Bullish crossover in oversold zone — strong buy signal
%K crosses below %D above 80
Bearish crossover in overbought zone — strong sell signal
Real example: Stock pulls back. Stochastic %K drops to 15. Then %K crosses above %D while both are still below 20. Many traders use this crossover combined with RSI to double-confirm a bounce entry before pulling the trigger.
8
Average True RangeATR Volatility★ Bounce
▼

Measures how much a stock typically moves per day on average. Tells you the expected range of movement. Does NOT tell you direction — only how big the moves tend to be. Critical for setting stop-losses and profit targets based on how volatile the stock actually is.

Parameters — ATR(period)
ATR(14)Default. Averages the true range over 14 periods. Most common setting across all platforms.
ATR(7)Shorter lookback — more reactive to recent volatility spikes. Used by day traders.
True Range formulaEach day = max of: (High − Low), (High − Prior Close), (Prior Close − Low). ATR is a smoothed average of these.
ATR = $1.50
Stock moves $1.50 on average per day. Use this to size stop-losses.
ATR spiking higher
Volatility expanding — bigger swings, higher risk, widen your stops
ATR declining
Volatility compressing — market calming down, consolidating
Stop = 1.5 × ATR below entry
Classic stop-loss formula — gives room to breathe without being too wide
Real example: You enter a bounce at $50. ATR(14) = $2.00. Stop = $50 − (1.5 × $2.00) = $47.00. If you used a $0.25 stop on a $2 ATR stock you would get stopped out constantly by normal daily noise before the bounce even happens.
★ Bounce Trading: ATR is your risk management tool. Use it on every bounce trade to size your stop-loss. Never use a fixed dollar stop — let ATR tell you how much room the stock naturally needs.
9
On-Balance VolumeOBV Volume
▼

Keeps a running total of volume. Adds volume on up days, subtracts on down days. The idea: volume precedes price. Smart money accumulates quietly before a move, which shows in OBV before price reacts. OBV rising while price falls = bullish divergence = bounce often follows.

How OBV is calculated — no parameters to set:
Price up todayOBV = Yesterday's OBV + Today's volume
Price down todayOBV = Yesterday's OBV − Today's volume
Price unchangedOBV = Yesterday's OBV (no change)

The actual OBV number is not meaningful. Only whether it is trending up or down relative to price direction is what matters.

OBV rising + price rising
Confirmed uptrend — buying is strong and real
OBV falling + price rising
Divergence — move may be weak, distribution happening
OBV rising + price falling
Bullish divergence — quiet accumulation, bounce likely ahead
OBV falling + price falling
Confirmed downtrend — selling pressure is real
Real example: Stock price drops for a week but OBV climbs slowly throughout. This means buyers are accumulating shares even as price falls — often a precursor to a strong reversal upward. OBV divergence often shows the bounce before price shows it.

Average Directional IndexADX Trend★ Bounce
▼

Measures how STRONG a trend is — but NOT which direction. A high ADX + price going up = strong uptrend. A high ADX + price going down = strong downtrend. A low ADX means no trend — the stock is ranging sideways. This tells you which mode the market is in, so you pick the right strategy.

Parameters — ADX(period)
ADX(14)Default. Comes with +DI and -DI lines that show which direction the trend is moving.
+DIPositive directional indicator — strength of upward movement
-DINegative directional indicator — strength of downward movement
ADX below 20
No real trend — stock is ranging. Oscillators like RSI work better here.
ADX above 25
Trend confirmed and strong. Trend-following strategies perform better.
ADX above 40
Very strong trend — stay with it until ADX starts declining
ADX falling from high
Trend is losing steam — possible reversal starting
Real example: RSI hits 28 on a stock. Looks like a great bounce setup. But ADX is 47 and still rising. The downtrend is extremely powerful. Even oversold RSI may not stop a strong downtrend. ADX tells you when NOT to trade a bounce as much as when to enter one.
★ Bounce Trading: Check ADX before entering. If ADX is above 35 and still rising, the trend is too strong to trade against — skip it. Look for bounce setups where ADX is below 25 or declining from a high peak, signaling the trend is exhausting.
11
Volume Weighted Average PriceVWAP Volume
▼

The average price of a stock weighted by volume throughout the trading day. Resets at the open every single day. Institutions use it as their daily benchmark — buying below VWAP means they got a good price for the session.

VWAP has no user-set period — it resets and recalculates automatically each day:
VWAP formula(Cumulative Price × Volume) ÷ Cumulative Volume — running total recalculated throughout each session.
Anchored VWAPAdvanced variant. You set the start point yourself (e.g., from a major swing low) instead of from the day open.
Price above VWAP
Bullish intraday bias — buyers in control today
Price below VWAP
Bearish intraday bias — sellers in control today
Price reclaims VWAP
Potential intraday reversal — watch for volume confirmation
Price hugging VWAP all day
Indecisive session — wait for a clear directional break
Real example: Stock opens weak and drops below VWAP by 10am. By noon it rallies back above VWAP on rising volume. Institutions see the stock as fairly priced again and begin buying. That VWAP reclaim is one of the most reliable intraday signals for day traders.

Fibonacci RetracementFib Support/Resistance★ Bounce
▼

Draws horizontal lines at key percentage levels of a prior price move, based on the Fibonacci sequence from mathematics. These levels act as natural support and resistance zones where price frequently pauses, bounces, or reverses. Widely used and widely watched — their effectiveness partly comes from being a self-fulfilling prophecy since so many traders use them.

Key levels — no formula input. You pick the swing high and swing low on your chart:
23.6%Shallow retracement — weak pullback, trend still very strong. Common in very strong uptrends.
38.2%Moderate retracement — common bounce zone in healthy uptrends.
50.0%Not a true Fibonacci number but widely watched as a psychological midpoint level.
61.8%"The golden ratio" — the most powerful level. Major bounce and reversal zone.
78.6%Deep retracement — if price reaches here the original trend may be reversing entirely.
Price touches 38.2% and holds
Shallow pullback holding — strong trend, good bounce zone
Price touches 61.8% and holds
Golden ratio support — very high-probability bounce level
Price breaks below 61.8%
Deep correction — original trend may be over
Multiple levels stack up
"Confluence" — extremely strong support when Fib + EMA + S/R all align
Real example: Stock rallied from $40 to $80. Now pulling back. You draw Fibonacci from $40 to $80. The 61.8% level lands at $55.28. Price pulls back to $55, RSI hits 32, and volume dries up. Fib level + oversold RSI + low volume = high-probability bounce setup.
★ Bounce Trading: Draw Fibonacci from the most recent swing low to swing high. Watch 38.2%, 50%, and 61.8%. When price hits one of those AND RSI is oversold AND volume drops on the pullback — that is your zone to watch for a bounce entry.

Support & ResistanceS/R Support/Resistance★ Bounce
▼

Horizontal price levels where a stock has repeatedly reversed or paused. Support is a floor where buyers stepped in before. Resistance is a ceiling where sellers pushed price down before. No formula — identified by eye on the chart. One of the oldest tools in trading and still one of the most effective.

No parameters — identified visually. Key rules:
More touches = strongerA level touched 4 times is much stronger than one touched once. More history = more traders watching it.
Role reversalOld resistance that is broken often becomes new support. Old support that is broken becomes new resistance.
Round numbers$50, $100, $200 etc. act as psychological S/R levels because many orders cluster at these prices.
Price returns to old support
High-probability bounce zone — watch for confirmation candle with volume
Price breaks below support
Support failed — next support level below becomes the target
Old resistance becomes support
Role reversal — bullish. Former ceiling now holds as floor.
Old support becomes resistance
Role reversal — bearish. Former floor now acts as ceiling.
★ Bounce Trading: Every bounce trade is a bet that a support level will hold. S/R is the foundation. Layer RSI, volume, and ATR on top to confirm before entering.

Plots dots above or below price. Dots below = uptrend, dots above = downtrend. When price crosses the dots, the system reverses direction. Simple and visual — great as a trailing stop tool.

Parameters — PSAR(step, max)

0.02 (step)Starts at 0.02, increases by 0.02 every time a new high/low is set. Higher step = more sensitive, more signals.
0.2 (max)Maximum the acceleration can reach. Lower max = slower dots, fewer reversal signals.

▼

Calculated from yesterday's high, low, and close to project support and resistance levels for today. Widely used by day traders to plan entries and exits before the market even opens.

Formulas — no user input, uses prior day OHLC data:

S1= (2 × PP) − Prior High — first support below PP
R2 / S2R2 = PP + (Prior High − Prior Low) | S2 = PP − (Prior High − Prior Low)

Price falls below PP
Bearish day bias — watch for bounces at S1
Price touches S1 and holds
First support holding — common intraday bounce level
Price breaks through S2
Significant weakness — sellers firmly in control today
Real example: Before market open you calculate S1 = $148.50. Stock opens weak and drops toward it. RSI on the 5-minute chart hits 32. Price touches $148.52 and bounces. Classic pivot point + RSI bounce trade used by day traders every single session.

Section 5 — Trading Fundamentals & Must-Know Formulas
These fundamentals come directly from the beginner addition — essential math and concepts every trader needs to understand before touching indicators.

Price Change %

((New Price – Old Price) / Old Price) × 100

Example: $50 → $55 = (5/50) × 100 = 10% gain

Return %

((Sell Price – Buy Price) / Buy Price) × 100

Shows your gain relative to the capital you put in

Profit (Dollar)

(Sell Price – Buy Price) × Quantity

Example: Buy 10 @ $100, sell @ $105 = $5 × 10 = $50 profit

Break-Even Price

Total Cost / Quantity

The price you need to hit to cover your costs including fees

Risk/Reward Ratio

Potential Profit / Potential Loss

Example: Risk $2, target $6 = 1:3 R/R. Aim for 1:2 minimum.

ATR Stop-Loss

Entry Price − (ATR(14) × 1.5)

Example: Entry $50, ATR $2 → Stop at $47.00

Why beginners lose money — Using market orders during high volatility (slippage). Focusing on dollar moves instead of percentage moves (misjudged risk). Failing to set stop-losses and letting small losses grow. Overtrading without a plan. Chasing too many indicators before understanding price action.
Section 6 — Order Types
Order TypeWhat it doesWhen to use it
Market OrderExecutes immediately at the best available priceWhen you need to get in or out fast. Accepts slippage.
Limit OrderExecutes only at your specified price or betterWhen price control matters more than guaranteed fill. May not fill.
Stop LossTriggers when price moves against you to a set level — becomes a market orderEvery trade. Non-negotiable. Protects against unlimited loss.
Stop-LimitTriggers at stop price, then places a limit order at a specified priceWhen you want slippage protection but accept the risk of no fill.
Trailing StopMoves with price by a set amount — locks in profits as price risesWhen you want to ride a trend and protect gains automatically.
OCOOne-Cancels-Other — typically a profit target + stop loss linked togetherSetting both your exit target and your stop at the same time.
Bid vs Ask (Spread): Bid = highest price buyers offer. Ask = lowest price sellers accept. The difference is the spread. Tighter spreads = more liquid stock = better fills. Always trade liquid stocks — wide spreads eat into your profits before you even start.

Slippage: When you place a market order, you get whatever price is available — which may be worse than what you saw on screen, especially in fast markets or thinly traded stocks.
Section 7 — Minimal Setup Strategy (Beginner Framework)

Step 1 — Trend (Moving Average)

Use SMA(50) or SMA(200) to identify market direction. Only trade in the direction of the trend. Do not fight the trend.

Step 2 — Momentum (RSI or Stochastic)

In an uptrend, watch for RSI rising from oversold. In a downtrend, watch for it falling from overbought. Match momentum to trend.

Step 3 — Confirmation (Volume or MACD)

Confirm entries with increasing volume or a MACD crossover. This filters out false signals before you commit capital.

Step 4 — Risk Management (ATR)

Set stop-loss at 1.5 × ATR(14) below entry. Size your position so a full stop-loss costs you no more than 1–2% of your account.

Section 8 — Complete Bounce Trading System

The 6-Step Bounce Trading Checklist

1
Qualify the setup first — check ADX(14)
If ADX is above 35 and still rising, the downtrend is too powerful to trade against. Skip this setup and wait for the next one.
2
Find the support zone
Look for price at a key level: EMA(20), EMA(50), SMA(200), Fibonacci 61.8%, or a clearly established horizontal support level. The stronger and more-watched the level, the better.
3
Confirm oversold with RSI(14)
RSI should be below 30 and ideally starting to curl upward. Add Stoch(14,3,3) below 20 with a %K/%D crossover as additional confirmation. Both below their thresholds = stronger signal.
4
Confirm momentum shift with MACD(12,26,9)
Look for the MACD line to cross above its signal line while both are still below zero. The histogram bars should be shrinking before the cross happens. This is your momentum trigger.
5
Confirm with volume
The reversal candle must show noticeably higher volume than the down candles that came before it. Low volume on a bounce = fake-out. High volume = commitment from buyers. Do not skip this step.
6
Size your risk with ATR(14)
Place your stop-loss at 1.5 × ATR(14) below your entry. Size your position so a full stop-out costs you no more than 1–2% of your trading account. Never use a fixed dollar stop — let ATR define the distance.
Trader consensus: No single indicator is required or foolproof. Most professionals use RSI + MACD + Moving Averages as a core trio, then add 1–2 more based on their personal style. Indicators work best when combined — one for trend, one for momentum, one for volume confirmation. No indicator combination eliminates risk. Always manage risk first.
